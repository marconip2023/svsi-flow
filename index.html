Supplier Voluntary Self-Inspection, step by step

SVSI is moving from a Word-and-email process onto the ICW platform. One task covers one factory, and it runs through eight steps and four roles before that factory is allowed to inspect its own products. This page follows the designed screens in order. Every screen opens the real frame in Figma, and every ▶ button plays that part of the flow as a clickable Figma prototype.

Before the task exists

QT

SVSI applies to a factory, not a supplier. So the buyer starts from the supplier list, expands a supplier to see the factories underneath it, and decides which of those factories are in scope. Adding a factory to SVSI puts it at Not Rated; the scheme is set after the audit. Factories that will never join are marked Out of Scope.

Application

Supplier appliesQT checks

The supplier starts the application themselves. This is the part that used to be the Word Application Form v4. They confirm the factory, run a self-assessment, pick the items the factory wants to self-inspect, and give payer details. The buyer then reviews the whole submission and either approves it, returns it for correction, or rejects it.

04

QT Review, Documents & Preview

QT

QT reads the lab's report and result. If it needs work, QT requests a revised report or rejects the task. Otherwise QT does everything in one pass: confirms the result with a remark and the item list, generates the Agreement and the Addendum, then previews all of it before sending. From the preview QT sends the task either straight to the SVSI team or to the ethical team first.

06

SVSI Team Review

SVSI Team

The SVSI team approves once. They see everything QT prepared and can return the task to QT or to the ethical team, or reject it. When they confirm, a preview shows QT's remark, documents, item list, Agreement and Addendum. If something is off, they leave a comment on the item (visible to QT only) instead of sending it back. Confirming sends the documents to the supplier.

Reference

The scheme ladder is a factory ladder

Products never carry a tier. They carry only New or Repeat.

TierHow a factory gets thereUL assessmentNew item uploadsRepeat item uploads
BRONZEFirst year in the programmeRequiredReport + Addendum—
SILVEROne good year at BronzeRequiredReport + AddendumReport + Addendum
GOLDOne good year at SilverNot requiredReport + AddendumAddendum only

So a Silver factory with a Repeat product still goes through the full lab flow. Repeat only lightens the document burden, and only at Gold does it drop the self-inspection report. An item graduates to Repeat by staying in the Addendum; drop it for a year and it comes back as New.

Open questions for the team

Things the design cannot settle on its own.

  • What can TBG do with each scheme?The scheme tabs (Not Rated, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Out of Scope) filter the list today. Once a tier is picked, which actions should each one offer?
  • How should the Factory and Supplier tables sort?Supplier by name A to Z and Factory by SVSI Task Status, or a Create Date column on both, latest first, like the Audit List.
  • Seasonal items are picked per WIC but written per category.The item list selects Seasonal items individually; the Addendum lists Seasonal only as a Product Category. Is that roll-up automatic, or does the buyer choose the categories again when generating?
  • Validity starts from the Addendum Effective Date, not the completion date.In the signed Greatstar example both fall on the same day. If they can differ, which one drives the validity window?
  • Lab wording is still UL-specific in places."UL Audit" and "Factory previous UL assessment date" remain, but BV can now be selected, so these need neutral wording or a rule.
